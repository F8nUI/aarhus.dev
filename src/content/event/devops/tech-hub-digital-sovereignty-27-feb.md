At this Morning Meetup, Professor Christian Damsgaard Jensen from Aarhus University presents the different aspects of digital sovereignty that must be considered by organisations that wish to transition away from traditional Big Tech providers towards EU-based providers of technology, infrastructure and services.

Rasmus Seeberg, VP of Engineering at iPaper, will present the motivations for, considerations before, and experiences from their migration of selected services from AWS to European providers aligned with the EU digital sovereignty agenda.

The Morning Meetup will also include a short introduction to TechCircle by Jesper Askov Bonde Petersen, Project Manager at Aarhus University’s Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering.

Achieving digital sovereignty for organisations in the EU means reducing dependence on non-EU providers across every layer that matters. This expands beyond choosing an EU-based data centre to understanding who can be legally compelled to hand over your data, how locked into a vendor's ecosystem you might become and how hard it would be to leave later.

It must also consider whether the software stack is open and auditable versus proprietary and opaque, whether critical infrastructure such as cloud, network routing and hardware has realistic EU or open-source alternatives with adequate support and talent behind them, and how encryption keys and access controls are managed so no external party holds a backdoor.

In practice, this is not just about full self-sufficiency, but also about identifying your highest-risk dependencies and prioritising migration or diversification there first, accepting some trade-offs in cost and convenience for meaningfully reduced strategic exposure.
